# 5. L2 Regularization (Weight Decay)

L2 regularization adds a penalty for large weights.

Modified loss:


L_total = L_data + λ Σ W²


Where:


λ = regularization strength


---

## Intuition

Large weights create **complex decision boundaries**.

L2 encourages:


smaller weights
simpler model


---

## Effect on Gradient

During backpropagation:


∂L/∂W = ∂L_data/∂W + λW


This slightly pulls weights toward zero.

---

## Toy Example (Using Our Network)

Original weight:


w1 = 0.628


Gradient:


dL/dw1 = -1.28


L2 penalty:


λ = 0.01


Gradient becomes:


-1.28 + 0.01(0.628)

≈ -1.2737


Updated weight:


w1 = w1 − lr × gradient


Regularization gently reduces weight magnitude.

---

# 6. L1 Regularization

Loss becomes:


L_total = L_data + λ Σ |W|


---

## Effect

Encourages **sparse weights**.

Meaning:


many weights become exactly zero


This effectively performs **feature selection**.

---

## Difference from L2

| L1 | L2 |
|----|----|
| Produces sparse weights | Produces small weights |
| Feature selection | Smooth weight decay |

---

# 7. Dropout

Dropout randomly **turns off neurons during training**.

Example network:


Layer1: ● ● ● ●
Dropout: ● X ● X
Layer2: ● ●


Where:


X = neuron temporarily disabled


---

## Why This Works

Without dropout:


neurons rely on each other too much


With dropout:


each neuron must learn useful features independently


This improves generalization.

---

## Typical Dropout Values

| Layer Type | Dropout |
|-------------|---------|
| Hidden layers | 0.5 |
| CNN layers | 0.2–0.3 |

---

# 8. Early Stopping

Another form of regularization.

Idea:


Stop training before overfitting begins


Training behavior:

# 11. Interview Explanation

**What is regularization in machine learning?**

> Regularization refers to techniques that reduce overfitting by constraining the model's complexity, encouraging it to learn general patterns rather than memorizing training data.

# 12. Interview Questions

### Q1

Why does L2 regularization work?

Answer:

It penalizes large weights, leading to simpler models and smoother decision boundaries.

---

### Q2

Difference between L1 and L2?

Answer:

L1 encourages sparsity (weights become zero), while L2 shrinks weights smoothly.

---

### Q3

Why does dropout improve generalization?

Answer:

It prevents neurons from co-adapting by randomly disabling them during training.

# 10. Model Generalization

## Overfitting
When the model memorizes training data instead of learning patterns.

**Symptoms**
- Very low training loss  
- High validation/test loss  

**Example**
- Deep neural network with too many parameters trained too long  

## Underfitting
When the model is too simple to capture patterns.

**Symptoms**
- High training loss  
- High test loss  

**Example**
- Linear model trying to fit non-linear data  

## Regularization Techniques (to improve generalization)

Regularization refers to techniques used to reduce overfitting by adding constraints or penalties to the model, ensuring it generalizes well to unseen data.

Key Idea
Prevents the model from becoming too complex and memorizing training data.
Achieved by adding a penalty term to the loss (error) function.

**L1 Regularization (Lasso)**  
Adds penalty term:  
$$
\lambda \sum |W|
$$  
Encourages sparse weights (feature selection).  

**L2 Regularization (Ridge)**  
Adds penalty term:  
$$
\lambda \sum W^2
$$  
Prevents very large weights, stabilizes training.  

**Dropout**  
- Randomly drops neurons during training  
- Prevents co-dependency among neurons  

**Data Augmentation**  
- Artificially expand dataset (rotate/flip images, add noise, etc.)  
- Improves robustness  

**Early Stopping**  
- Monitor validation loss  
- Stop training before overfitting kicks in  

## Interview Soundbite
“Overfitting means the model memorizes the training set, underfitting means it is too simple to even fit the training data. We fight overfitting with regularization methods like L1/L2, dropout, data augmentation, and early stopping.”
