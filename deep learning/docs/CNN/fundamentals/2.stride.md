# Stride in Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN)

Stride is one of the core parameters of the **convolution operation**.  
After understanding convolution, stride becomes very intuitive because it simply controls **how the filter moves across the image**.

Learning flow:

1. What stride means
2. Mental model
3. Visual intuition
4. Full numeric toy example
5. Impact on output size
6. Why stride is useful
7. Interview explanation

---

# 1. What is Stride?

Stride defines **how many pixels the filter moves at each step** while scanning the image.

Example:


Stride = 1


Filter moves **one pixel at a time**.


Stride = 2


Filter moves **two pixels at a time**.

---

# 2. Mental Model

Think of convolution like a **sliding window scanning the image**.


Image scanning

Stride = 1
step → step → step → step

Stride = 2
step →→ step →→ step


Larger stride means:

- fewer positions scanned
- smaller output feature map

---

# 3. Visual Intuition

Input image:


1 2 3 4
5 6 7 8
9 1 2 3
4 5 6 7


Filter size:


2 × 2


---

## Case 1 — Stride = 1

Filter moves **one pixel at a time**.

Positions visited:


[1 2]
[5 6]

[2 3]
[6 7]

[3 4]
[7 8]


Then next row.

This produces a **3 × 3 feature map**.

---

## Case 2 — Stride = 2

Filter jumps **two pixels each step**.

Positions visited:


[1 2]
[5 6]


Next position:


[3 4]
[7 8]


Then next row:


[9 1]
[4 5]


Next:


[2 3]
[6 7]


Output becomes **2 × 2 feature map**.

# 5. Output Size Formula

For convolution:


Output Size =
((Input − Kernel + 2 × Padding) / Stride) + 1


Example:

Input:


4 × 4


Kernel:


2 × 2


Stride:


2


Output:


((4 − 2) / 2) + 1 = 2


So feature map = **2 × 2**.

---

# 6. Why Stride is Important

Stride helps control:

### 1. Spatial Resolution

Large stride → smaller feature maps.

---

### 2. Computational Efficiency

Smaller feature maps mean:

- fewer calculations
- faster models

---

### 3. Downsampling

Stride can act like **automatic downsampling**.

Many modern CNNs use:


stride = 2


instead of pooling.

---

# 8. Interview Explanation

**What is stride in CNN?**

Good answer:

> Stride is the number of pixels a convolution filter moves across the input image at each step. A larger stride reduces the spatial size of the output feature map and decreases computational cost.

---

# 9. Common Interview Questions

### Q1  
What happens if stride increases?

Answer:

Output feature map becomes smaller.

---

### Q2  
What is the typical stride value?

Answer:

Most convolution layers use **stride = 1**, while **stride = 2** is used for downsampling.

---

### Q3  
What is the relationship between stride and pooling?

Answer:

Both reduce spatial dimensions. Modern architectures sometimes replace pooling with stride-2 convolutions.
