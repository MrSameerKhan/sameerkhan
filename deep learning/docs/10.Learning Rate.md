# 1. Why Learning Rate Exists

In training we update weights using gradients:


W_new = W_old − η × ∂L/∂W


Where:


η = learning rate


Learning rate controls **how large a step we take in parameter space**.

Think of it as the **step size while descending the loss landscape**.

---

# 2. Mental Model

Imagine standing on a mountain trying to reach the valley.


Loss = height
Parameters = position


You look at the slope (gradient) and step downhill.

Learning rate determines **how big the step is**.


Small step → slow progress
Large step → overshoot valley


---

# 3. What Happens if Learning Rate is Too Small

Example:


η = 0.000001


Update:


W_new = W_old − tiny_value


Effect:

- Training becomes extremely slow
- Model may take millions of iterations
- Sometimes it appears as if training is stuck

Visualization:


Iteration 1: small step
Iteration 2: small step
Iteration 10000: still far from minimum


---

# 4. What Happens if Learning Rate is Too Large

Example:


η = 10


Update:


W_new = W_old − 10 × gradient


Effect:

- Steps overshoot the minimum
- Loss oscillates
- Training may diverge

Visualization:


minimum
↓
step → overshoot → overshoot → overshoot

# 7. Learning Rate Schedules

Instead of keeping learning rate constant, we reduce it during training.

---

## Step Decay

Learning rate decreases after fixed intervals.

Example:


epoch 1–10: lr = 0.01
epoch 11–20: lr = 0.001
epoch 21–30: lr = 0.0001


Purpose:

- fast early learning
- stable later convergence

---

## Exponential Decay

Learning rate decreases exponentially.


lr = lr_initial × e^(−kt)


Used in many training pipelines.

---

## Cosine Decay

Learning rate follows cosine curve.


lr = lr_min + 0.5(lr_max − lr_min)(1 + cos(t))


Popular in modern deep learning training.

---

## Warmup

Start with very small learning rate and gradually increase.

Example:


epoch 1–5: lr increases
epoch 6+: normal lr


Used in Transformer training.

Reason:

Large gradients early in training can destabilize learning.

**What is learning rate?**

> The learning rate is a hyperparameter that controls how much model parameters are updated during each optimization step.

# 11. Interview Follow-Up Questions

### Q1

Why can't we set a very large learning rate?

Answer:

Large learning rates cause overshooting and unstable training.

---

### Q2

Why do we decay learning rate during training?

Answer:

Early training benefits from larger steps, while later stages require smaller steps to fine-tune parameters.

---

### Q3

Why do Transformers use learning rate warmup?

Answer:

Warmup prevents unstable updates caused by large gradients at the beginning of training.

# 8. Learning Rate Schedules

# Learning Rate

The learning rate tells the optimizer how big a step to take when updating the weights in the direction opposite to the gradient for a mini-batch.

## When the Learning Rate is Low
- Training is more stable and reliable.  
- Convergence is slow, since steps toward the minimum of the loss function are very small.  

## When the Learning Rate is High
- Training may fail to converge or even diverge.  
- Weight updates are too large, causing the optimizer to overshoot the minimum and make training unstable.  


## Why Schedules?
- A fixed learning rate (η) can be suboptimal.  
- Too high → divergence.  
- Too low → very slow convergence.  
- Solution: adjust η **over epochs** to balance exploration (high LR early) and convergence (low LR later).

## Common Schedules

**Step Decay**  
- Reduce η by a factor every k epochs.  
- Example: η ← η * 0.1 every 30 epochs.  
- Works well for classical CV tasks.

**Exponential Decay**  
- Smooth reduction each epoch.  
- η_t = η₀ * exp(-kt).  

**Cosine Annealing**  
- Learning rate follows a cosine curve.  
- Starts high, gradually decreases to near zero.  
- Often combined with **restarts** (Cosine Annealing with Warm Restarts, SGDR).  

**Warmup**  
- Start with a **small LR → gradually increase** for a few epochs.  
- Prevents instability early in training.  
- Widely used in **transformers and large models**.  


## Interview Soundbite
“Learning rate schedules let us start with a high learning rate to explore the parameter space, then gradually reduce it to converge stably. Cosine annealing with warmup is the de-facto choice for transformers today.”
