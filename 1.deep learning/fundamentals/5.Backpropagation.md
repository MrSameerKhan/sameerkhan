

# 6. Backpropagation

**Core idea**: Chain rule of calculus.  

**Steps**
1. Compute loss at output.  
2. Propagate gradients backward ($\partial L / \partial W$).  
3. Update weights:  
   $$
   W := W - \eta \cdot \frac{\partial L}{\partial W}
   $$
   where $\eta$ = learning rate.  

**Problems**
- **Vanishing gradients**: sigmoid/tanh shrink gradients → slow learning.  
- **Exploding gradients**: large weights → unstable training.  


# Backpropagation (Backprop)

##  What is it?

Backpropagation is the algorithm for training neural networks.  
It computes gradients of the loss w.r.t. weights and biases using the **chain rule of calculus**,  
then updates the weights via **gradient descent**.  



## Training Pipeline (Big Picture)

**Forward Pass → compute predictions**

$$
\hat{y} = f(Wx + b)
$$

**Loss Function → compare with true labels**

$$
L(\hat{y}, y)
$$

**Backward Pass (Backprop) → compute gradients**

$$
\frac{\partial L}{\partial W}, \quad \frac{\partial L}{\partial b}
$$

**Update Weights → Gradient Descent**

$$
W \leftarrow W - \eta \cdot \frac{\partial L}{\partial W}
$$

$$
b \leftarrow b - \eta \cdot \frac{\partial L}{\partial b}
$$



## Core Idea: Chain Rule

If we have:  

$$
z = Wx + b, \quad y = f(z), \quad L = loss(y, y_{true})
$$

Then by chain rule:

$$
\frac{\partial L}{\partial W} = 
\frac{\partial L}{\partial y} \cdot
\frac{\partial y}{\partial z} \cdot
\frac{\partial z}{\partial W}
$$



## Backprop in Multi-Layer Network

For a **2-layer MLP**:

**Hidden layer**

$$
h = f(W_1 x + b_1)
$$

**Output layer**

$$
\hat{y} = g(W_2 h + b_2)
$$

**Loss**

$$
L(\hat{y}, y)
$$

**Backprop steps**

- Compute output error:

$$
\delta_{out} = \frac{\partial L}{\partial \hat{y}} \cdot g'(z_2)
$$

- Gradients for output weights:

$$
\frac{\partial L}{\partial W_2} = \delta_{out} \cdot h^T
$$

- Propagate error to hidden:

$$
\delta_{hidden} = (W_2^T \cdot \delta_{out}) \cdot f'(z_1)
$$

- Gradients for hidden weights:

$$
\frac{\partial L}{\partial W_1} = \delta_{hidden} \cdot x^T
$$


## Why Backprop is Important

- **Efficient**: avoids recomputing derivatives from scratch.  
- Works for **any network depth**.  
- Core of modern optimizers (SGD, Adam, etc.).  

##  Interview Soundbite

“Backpropagation is the process of calculating gradients of the loss with respect to each parameter in a neural network using the chain rule. We start from the output layer and move backward, layer by layer, to update weights efficiently with gradient descent.”
